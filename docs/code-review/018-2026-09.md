## Review summary

Detection is in reasonable shape since the `3cb467d` rewrite and the Cycle 16 aim fix. In the
00:49 session 25% of pursuit scans were locked, against 5.8% pooled before the acquisition region
was widened. 85% of pursuits had a lock at some point, and lock runs lasted a median of 9 ticks
(longest 96), against a median of 2 before. There were 0 lock ticks in an excluded HUD zone.

The largest remaining gains are in actuation and chase safety, not detection. The pitch axis
overshoots the target most of the time, and the steering law can settle on a target directly
below the nose and push into a dive.

One suspicion was checked and ruled out (**measured**): the pursuit loop presses fire every cycle
whether or not the tracker has a lock. Across all logs, 35 pursuits with no tracker lock spent 0
missiles, and 21 pursuits with a lock spent 27. The game launches only on its own lock, so the
constant fire press costs no ammunition.

### CR-018-01 — Pitch overshoots through the deadband (High)

**Measured (00:49 session).** Of 513 `HOLD[pitch]` endings, 324 (63%) are a direct reversal
(`up -> down` 162, `down -> up` 162) that never passes through the deadband. Roll reverses directly
in 80 of 382 endings (21%). On lock ticks the steering point's |dy| median is 104 px and only 18%
of lock ticks sit inside the pitch deadband (0.05, about 30 px either side of centre at 1920 x 1200).
Signed dy is wide on both sides (10th to 90th percentile -161 to +262 px, median +30), so this is
oscillation, not a fixed bias.

**Measured.** The median interval between `TRACKPICK` ticks is 0.365 s (10th percentile 0.341 s),
about 2.7 Hz. `PURSUIT SUMMARY` lines agree (for example 60 scans in 20.1 s).

**Mechanism (code).** `_sustained_pitch_hold` holds the key until a later tick sees the error inside
the deadband. The pursuit loop ([controller.py `pursue_and_engage`](../../wingman/controller.py))
waits `_eject_stop.wait(timeout=0.2)` and then calls
`fire_active_weapon(hold_seconds=0.1, block=True)` every cycle, so each cycle spends at least 0.3 s
not steering.

**Inferred.** At 2.7 Hz the nose travels well past a 60 px band between samples, so a sustained
hold turns into a limit cycle.

**Proposed fix.**

1. Make the fire press non-blocking (`block=False`) or move it to its own thread, and shorten the
   loop wait to about 0.1 s. Firing costs nothing without a game lock (see the summary), so its
   cadence can be decoupled from steering.
2. Damp the pitch channel. Either smooth the steering point (an alpha-beta filter) and release the
   hold when the predicted error at the next tick is inside the deadband, or use the existing tap
   mode (`sustained_hold=False`) when |err_y| is small and the sustained hold only above a band.

**Verdict criteria for the live check.** The direct-reversal share of `HOLD[pitch]` endings falls
well below 63%; the share of lock ticks inside the pitch deadband rises above 18%; the tick interval
median falls below 0.2 s. `make sr` already reports the deadband shares.

### CR-018-02 — The steering law can settle on a target below and push into a dive (High)

**Mechanism (code).** Roll acts only on `error_norm` (horizontal) and pitch only on `error_norm_y`
(vertical). Rolling rotates the target about the screen centre, so horizontal error is zero both
with the target at 12 o'clock and at 6 o'clock. At 6 o'clock nothing rolls and pitch commands nose
down, and nothing moves the target off 6 o'clock. Under this law it is a stable resting point.

**Measured.** On lock ticks the target is below centre 59% of the time; `HOLD[pitch]` pressed
nose-down 310 times against nose-up 261. The action item's 01:43 entry counts six of eight
unplanned deaths in the session as following a chase dive through the 3000 m floor. The 01:37 impact
starts with `HOLD[pitch]: None -> down (target err_y=+0.601)` and no release before the ground.

**Inferred.** The chase's dives are at least partly a product of the steering law, not only of
where targets happen to be.

**Proposed change (behaviour change; operator's call).** Lift-vector pursuit: compute the target's
angle around the screen centre, roll to bring it to 12 o'clock, and pull nose-up when it is within a
tolerance of 12 o'clock. Allow only small nose-down corrections near centre. The altitude guard
then becomes a single rule: below the doctrine floor plus a margin, limit bank so the lift vector
stays above the horizon (or decline targets below). This would take the place of the preventive
dive guard proposed in action item 001 (01:06 and 01:43 entries).

**Assumed, needs a live comparison.** That the game's flight model turns faster under positive G
than under a push, and that the camera rolls with the aircraft (the current roll law already relies
on the latter).

### CR-018-03 — Our lock is not the game's lock (Medium)

**Measured.** Launches happen only when the game locks (see the summary), so `locked=` in
`PURSUIT SUMMARY` measures nameplate visibility, not engagement.

The game draws a lock-status text at a fixed position that the tracker currently only masks out
(`tracking.red_mass_exclude_pct`, the "NO LOCK" text). Reading it would give a direct per-pursuit
outcome (time to first game lock, share of time game-locked), which is the right target for
tuning CR-018-01 and CR-018-02.

**Assumed, to check on archived frames first.** That the text changes visibly when the game locks.

### CR-018-04 — Three generations of tracker in one code path (Medium)

**Measured.** With the shipped config (`red_mass_steering: true`, `red_mass_cluster_select: true`,
`red_mass_nameplate_gate_enabled: true`, `red_mass_tallbar_fallback: false`) the tall-bar detector
never steers, yet every tick still runs it: `_detect_targets` builds two HSV masks and contours over
the whole acquisition region, `_select_target` and the Selection Hardening Phase 1 and 2 shadow
logs run on its output, and the crop is converted to HSV a second time in `_red_mass_probe`. The
00:49 log has 3,594 `path=suppressed` ticks: the tall-bar path found a candidate and was vetoed.

**Measured.** Code defaults differ from shipped config for `red_mass_steering`,
`red_mass_nameplate_gate_enabled`, `red_mass_tallbar_fallback`, `red_mass_cluster_select`,
`acquisition_region_pct` and `red_mass_exclude_zones_pct`. Most synthetic tests in
`tests/test_target_tracking.py` build a bare config, so they exercise a tracker that does not ship,
and a config without these keys silently gets the old behaviour.

**Also.** The module and class docstrings still describe green and red tall bars, and `update()`'s
thread note (see CR-018-05) is inaccurate.

**Proposed fix.** Remove the tall-bar path, `_select_target_ranked` and both shadow logs,
`_red_mass_centroid` and the non-cluster gate branch; make the shipped values the code defaults;
move the remaining tests onto the shipped config. `n_detections` and the `tall=` / `n_tall=` /
`red_won=` fields of `TRACKPICK` go with it, so `scripts/session-report.py` needs checking.
Performance gain is small (the tracker measured 3.7 to 9.5 ms per tick); the gain is one code path
that matches what ships.

### CR-018-05 — `TargetTracker` is driven from three threads (Low)

**Code.** The class docstring says it is not thread-safe and must be called only from the main
loop. It is called from the main tick (`TrackingHandler.tick`, in `GAME_BATTLE` and
`GAME_BATTLE_MANUAL`), from the pursuit thread and from the eject heatdive thread (both in
`GAME_BATTLE_EJECT`).

**Inferred, not observed.** Game state keeps them apart except at transitions: the main tick reads
its state once at the start of the tick, and the pursuit thread can run for up to 0.2 s after a
takeover sets `_eject_stop`. Two interleaved `update()` calls would corrupt `_mode`,
`_last_cluster` and the grace timer.

**Proposed fix.** A lock around `update()` and `reset()`, or an owner-thread check that logs and
skips a second caller.

### CR-018-06 — Target choice ignores range (Low, optional)

**Code.** On acquisition `_probe_by_cluster` chooses the eligible nameplate nearest the screen
centre. Nameplates carry the range as text (for example "10.3km"). OCR of the chosen cluster's crop
on the existing EasyOCR pool could prefer near contacts, skip ones out of missile range and, with
CR-018-02, decline targets well below when the aircraft is low.

**Inferred benefit only.** No measurement yet of how often a nearer target was on screen when a
farther one was chosen.
